## At-a-Glance Rules

- Participation is for University of Kelaniya students.
- Team size is 1-3 members: solo, duo, or team of three.
- The build sprint is online, with online training and mentorship; the finale is an in-person pitching event.
- A project must address one of six official tracks.
- AI must be central to the product, not an incidental add-on.
- The deck requires use of Alibaba Cloud AI tools and says the solution must be "powered by Qwenwork / Qwen ecosystem."
- The deliverable must be a functional, testable live prototype, not static wireframes or slides.
- Submission deliverables named by the deck are a GitHub repository, working demo video, and project documentation.
- The website names Qoder, QoderWork, and MuleRun as the event toolkit.
- The deck's submission deadline is August 31, 2026; the website's is August 27, 2026. This remains unresolved.
- The five judging criteria are published, but no weights or scoring scale are disclosed.

## Track 06 Deep-Read

The organizer's full Track 06 scope is:

> "Autonomous AI agents, omnichannel workflow automation, ticket resolution, and sentiment analysis."

The four phrases are best treated as the track's visible product surface. The concrete behaviors below are an implementation interpretation, not additional organizer rules.

### 1. Autonomous AI Agents

**What judges should be able to see:** A support request enters the system; the agent understands intent and context, retrieves grounded information, selects tools, performs permitted actions, verifies the result, and either resolves or escalates the request.

**Strong prototype surfaces:**

- Intent, entity, urgency, and account-context extraction rather than a generic chat reply.
- Retrieval from a cited knowledge base or policy source before answering.
- Tool use that changes real workflow state, such as looking up an order, checking an entitlement, updating a case, or issuing an approval request.
- Multi-step planning with visible action status and a final verification step.
- Confidence thresholds, policy checks, permission boundaries, and human approval for risky actions.
- A full audit trail that explains what evidence and tools led to the outcome.

**Rubric connection:** The agent loop and safeguards demonstrate AI depth; real tool execution and auditable state changes demonstrate technical execution. A scripted chatbot without retrieval or action-taking would show only a small part of this capability.

### 2. Omnichannel Workflow Automation

**What judges should be able to see:** The same customer conversation can arrive through multiple channels and continue as one coherent support case rather than becoming disconnected duplicate tickets.

**Strong prototype surfaces:**

- Ingestion adapters for at least two demonstrable channels, such as web chat and email; voice or social messaging can be represented by a realistic adapter if time permits.
- Customer identity and conversation-thread reconciliation across channels.
- A normalized event model feeding one workflow, queue, and customer history.
- Automatic acknowledgements, routing, assignment, follow-up, and SLA timers.
- Channel-appropriate responses while preserving shared case context.
- Duplicate detection and a timeline showing every inbound message, agent action, and handoff.

**Rubric connection:** A single live flow entering in one channel and resuming in another is an immediately legible demo moment. MuleRun is naturally relevant where models, channel APIs, ticket data, and workflow triggers need to be connected.

### 3. Ticket Resolution

**What judges should be able to see:** The system improves the full ticket lifecycle, not merely ticket creation or summarization.

**Strong prototype surfaces:**

- Triage, categorization, deduplication, priority prediction, queue routing, and SLA-risk detection.
- A grounded resolution plan assembled from customer context, policies, and prior successful cases.
- Execution of reversible low-risk actions, followed by confirmation that the customer's problem is solved.
- Explicit resolution states: resolved automatically, awaiting customer, awaiting approval, or escalated to a human.
- A concise handoff package containing the summary, evidence, attempted actions, sentiment history, and recommended next step.
- Outcome measures visible in the demo, such as time to resolution, avoided handoffs, automation rate, first-contact resolution, or SLA compliance.

**Rubric connection:** Closing the loop from intake through verified resolution makes the business value measurable and prevents the product from looking like another answer-generation interface.

### 4. Sentiment Analysis

**What judges should be able to see:** Sentiment changes routing or agent behavior; it is not just a decorative positive/neutral/negative badge.

**Strong prototype surfaces:**

- Per-message sentiment plus a trend across the complete conversation.
- Detection of frustration, urgency, churn risk, abusive content, or a failed-resolution pattern.
- Sentiment-aware prioritization, escalation, response tone, and supervisor alerts.
- Evidence highlighting the phrases that triggered the signal, with a confidence value.
- Language-aware behavior and an explicit fallback for uncertain classifications.
- A feedback mechanism allowing a human to correct the signal and preserve the correction for evaluation.

**Rubric connection:** The signal becomes meaningful AI integration when it drives an observable workflow decision. A strong demo can show identical ticket content taking a different route after an escalating customer reply.

### Integrated Track 06 Demo Shape

A convincing end-to-end demo would combine all four capabilities in one traceable scenario:

1. A customer starts in web chat and follows up by email.
2. The system links both messages to one customer and one ticket.
3. Sentiment and urgency worsen, changing priority and response policy.
4. An autonomous agent retrieves a cited policy, checks account or order state through a tool, and proposes or performs a safe resolution.
5. A risky action requests human approval; the handoff includes context, evidence, attempted actions, and sentiment history.
6. The system verifies the outcome, closes the ticket, and shows time saved or another concrete operational metric.

This is only a product interpretation. The published material does not require every item above, prescribe channels, identify a dataset, or define target metrics.

## Rubric-to-Evidence Checklist

Use this as a submission and demo quality gate:

| Criterion | Evidence the prototype should make visible |
|---|---|
| Innovation & Originality | A specific underserved support failure; a distinct mechanism beyond generic RAG chat; evidence explaining why the combination or workflow is novel |
| AI Integration & Depth | Grounded retrieval, model reasoning/classification, multi-step tool use, confidence handling, evaluation results, and meaningful sentiment-driven decisions |
| Technical Execution & Architecture | Stable live path, clear data and agent boundaries, Alibaba Cloud/Qwen ecosystem usage, logs, safe failure modes, scalability rationale, and reproducible setup |
| Impact & Business Feasibility | Named buyer/user, baseline pain, measurable operational outcome, adoption path, risk controls, and credible ROI assumptions |
| Pitch & Demo Delivery | One short end-to-end scenario showing all four track surfaces, a reliable fallback recording/path, clear before/after result, and prepared Q&A on safety and economics |

This checklist is analytical guidance derived from the rubric wording; it is not an official scoring formula.
